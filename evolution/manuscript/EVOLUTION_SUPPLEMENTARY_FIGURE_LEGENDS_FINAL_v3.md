# Supplementary evolutionary figure legends — final v2

**Supplementary Figure S1. Representative vertebrate phylogeny of the S9 prolyl-peptidase family.** Maximum-likelihood tree of 96 representative vertebrate S9-family proteins spanning the nine human paralog groups. Branch lengths indicate amino-acid substitutions per site. This broader tree supports orthology assignments and paralog relationships used in the focused evolutionary analyses; composition-sensitive deep branching is not used to infer the root of the family.

**Supplementary Figure S2. Beta-propeller-domain sensitivity analysis of the FAP-DPP4 lineage.** Maximum-likelihood phylogeny constructed from the beta-propeller region of the focused FAP/DPP4 boundary dataset. Internal labels indicate SH-aLRT/UFBoot support where available. The lungfish FAP sequence remains FAP-like, and teleost FAP and DPP4 copies form lineage-specific subclades, supporting the principal full-length analysis.

**Supplementary Figure S3. Catalytic-domain sensitivity analysis of the FAP-DPP4 lineage.** Maximum-likelihood phylogeny constructed from the C-terminal catalytic region. The catalytic-domain tree retains the lineage-specific teleost signal and places the gar-like ancestral branch basal to the four teleost copies with strong support, consistent with duplication after the gar split.

**Supplementary Figure S4. Statistical comparison of alternative FAP-DPP4 evolutionary topologies.** Delta log-likelihood values are shown relative to the best tree. A minimal lineage-specific duplication model is indistinguishable from the unconstrained maximum-likelihood optimum, whereas a topology forcing teleost fap and dpp4 into ancient orthology with tetrapod FAP and DPP4 is strongly rejected by the approximately unbiased test.

**Supplementary Figure S5. Maximum-likelihood ancestral-state reconstruction at the FAP A657-equivalent specificity position.** Posterior probabilities are shown for the principal ancestral states. The broader/preduplication FAP-DPP4 lineage and gar/teleost states strongly favor Asp, whereas the lungfish/tetrapod FAP ancestor strongly favors Ala (posterior probability 0.99392), supporting a D-to-A specificity transition on the sarcopterygian FAP stem.

**Supplementary Figure S6. Maximum-likelihood ancestral-state reconstruction at the DPP4-S630-equivalent catalytic position in the DPP6-DPP10 lineage.** The deeper DPP6/DPP10-family precursor strongly favors Ser (posterior probability 0.9798). The vertebrate DPP6/DPP10 ancestral node also favors Ser (0.80255), with Ala as the principal alternative (0.18624), whereas the modern DPP6 and DPP10 crowns strongly favor Asp (0.99621) and Gly (0.99556), respectively. The reconstruction establishes catalytic-site remodeling in the extant paralogs but does not resolve a specific intermediate S-to-A-to-D/G substitution trajectory.

**Supplementary Figure S7. Focused full-length maximum-likelihood phylogeny of the FAP-DPP4 boundary dataset.** Maximum-likelihood tree of 21 FAP/DPP4-lineage proteins inferred from the 776-aa trimmed full-length alignment using IQ-TREE 3.1.4 with the Q.PFAM+F+R5 model and 1,000 SH-aLRT plus 1,000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates. The inference was performed unrooted and is displayed with the Ciona FAP/DPP4-lineage sequence F6SZT6 as an outgroup for visualization. The focused tree supports lineage-specific resolution of FAP/DPP4 relationships across bony vertebrates and provides the phylogenetic framework used for the topology, synteny and ancestral-state analyses.
